ServeTrack Handbook

Jobs, service attempts, affidavits and billing for a process serving company — from the phone in your pocket. Sign in at servetrack.onrender.com.

Getting in

Set up your account with an access code everyone
  1. Open servetrack.onrender.com and tap "Set up your account" under the sign-in form.
  2. Type the access code you were given, your name, email, and a password of at least 8 characters.
  3. That's it — you're in, and from then on you sign in with just the email and password.

Forgot your password? Your administrator can set you a new one from Setup; use it to sign in, then change it under Setup → My account.

Running the company

For administrators. Your company's jobs, clients and billing are yours alone — nobody outside your company can see them.

Create a job from the court papers admin
  1. Jobs → + New job.
  2. Upload the summons or citation PDF. The case number, court, parties and service address read themselves into the form. (A photo-scan can't be read — the app will say so, and you type it in instead.)
  3. Pick the client, the fee, the server's pay, and who's serving it.
  4. Save. The job numbers itself (ST-10001, ST-10002…) and shows up on that server's My Day immediately.
Add people to your team admin
  1. Setup → Access codes → create a code (choose Field server or Admin, set their default pay per serve).
  2. Text them the code — they set up their own account with it and land on your team.

Or add them yourself under Setup → Add person. Deactivating someone (Setup → their name → Deactivated) keeps their history but ends their access.

Affidavit templates admin

Setup → Templates. Write the affidavit your courts expect once, with merge fields like {{recipient_name}}, {{served_date}} and {{attempts_list}} where the real details go. Every served job can then print a finished affidavit — details, attempt history and GPS merged in — ready for the notary. Any photos your servers took print behind it as Exhibit A, each one captioned with the attempt it belongs to and the date and time it was taken. Mark one template as the default; keep others for different jurisdictions.

Bill clients, pay servers admin
  1. Bill → Invoices: pick a client and a date range. Every served, un-invoiced job in the period lines itself up — preview, create, print, and mark Paid when the check clears.
  2. Bill → Statements: the same for what you owe a server — their completed serves gathered into a printable pay statement.

A job can only ever sit on one invoice and one statement, so nothing is billed twice. Both print under your company's name.

Working the field

For field servers.

Your day server
  1. My Day lists your open jobs, rush work first. Tap an address for turn-by-turn directions in your maps app.
  2. At the door: open the job → Log attempt → pick what happened — Served, No Answer, Bad Address, Moved, Refused, Evading.
  3. Your GPS position is stamped on every attempt. That's not surveillance — it's what makes the affidavit hold up when service is challenged.
  4. Take photos. Tap ＋ Photo on the attempt — the door, the house number, a posted notice, the car in the drive. Your phone opens the camera; the app shrinks each shot before sending it, so it works on a weak signal. Add a caption if it needs one.
  5. An outcome of Served completes the job: note who accepted it and how, and a short description of the person.

Forgot to photograph something? Open the job later and tap ＋ Photo on that attempt — photos can be added, captioned or deleted any time.

Scan instead of search server

Every printed cover sheet carries a barcode. Open the Scan tab, point the camera at it, and the job opens itself — no typing job numbers in a parking lot.

Print address labels anyone
  1. Tools tab → pick the jobs to label.
  2. The app knows which labels on your current sheet are already used and prints only onto the free ones — so a part-used sheet goes back in the printer and keeps working until it's gone.
  3. Loaded a new sheet? Tap Fresh sheet first.

Good to know

The first open of the day can be slow. After a quiet spell the service takes up to a minute to wake. Once it's awake, it's quick all day.
Your password is yours to change. Setup → My account. You'll need your current password to set a new one.

What ServeTrack stores and who can see it is written plainly in the privacy statement — including the GPS on attempts, the photos your servers take, and exactly what the platform operator can and cannot see of your company's cases. Photos are the one thing the operator is refused outright, even in support view: a face at a door cannot be blurred into a shape the way a name can.
